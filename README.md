# Secure Life General Hospital Management System and Analysis
I used Microsoft Excel to analyse 1,000 patient records for 2024–2025 and develop an interactive dashboard, providing actionable insights and recommendations to improve operational efficiency, financial accountability and healthcare service delivery at Secure Life General Hospital.

In order to evaluate its operational and financial performance and to provide actionable recommendations for improved operational efficiency, financial accountability and healthcare service delivery, the management of Secure Life General Hospital engaged my services to develop a Hospital Management System Dashboard and to answer some business questions.

## **Dataset Description**

The analysis was conducted using a two year dataset covering the 2024–2025 reporting period, provided by the hospital management.

The dataset comprised 1,000 patient records across 10 variables, including Patient ID, Patient Name, Age, Gender, Admission Date, Discharge Date, Department, Diagnosis, Total Bill and Insurance Coverage Percentage. 

## **Methodology**

Microsoft Excel was used for data cleaning, analysis and dashboard development.

The analytical process involved:

- Data cleaning and preparation to ensure data consistency and accuracy.
- Creation of calculated columns to derive relevant performance indicators. 
- Development of PivotTables to summarise and analyse key operational and financial metrics.
- Creation of interactive charts and visualisations.

## **Key Findings**
The analysis revealed that

For Patient Visits and Financial Performance, the hospital recorded a total of 1,000 patient visits, generating approximately $8.05 million in total bills. Of this amount, $4.57 million (57%) was recorded as paid, while $3.48 million (43%) remained outstanding.

For Patient Length of Stay and Insurance Coverage, the average patient length of stay was five days, while the average insurance coverage stood at 75%. This is useful in determining patient utilisation patterns and the role of insurance in healthcare financing.

For Patient Demographic Analysis, Female patients accounted for 51% of total patient records, while male patients represented 49%, indicating a relatively balanced gender distribution.

In terms of age distribution, adults aged 36–59 years constituted the largest patient group, accounting for 37% of total patients, followed by elderly patients aged 60 years and above, who represented 31%.

For Departmental Performance, analysis revealed that while General Medicine recorded the highest number of patient visits, accounting for 25% of total patients, Orthopaedics generated the largest share of the hospital's total bills, contributing 33%, hightlighting differences in patient volume and billing contributions across departments. 

Further analysis of insurance coverage across departments and diagnoses identified General Medicine as having the highest recorded insurance value ($186.20), while Pneumonia recorded the highest insurance value among diagnoses ($33.10).

Analysis of quarterly admission and billing trends across 2024 and 2025 revealed fluctuations in both patient admissions and revenue generation. This highlights the importance of continuously monitoring patient demand and financial performance to identify emerging trends, anticipate periods of increased demand and support effective resource allocation and operational planning.

## **Limitation**

It is pertinent to note that, the dataset did not clearly distinguish between the actual amounts received and outstanding balances for partially paid bills. This limits the accuracy of the reported payment and outstanding figures and highlights the need for improved billing records.

## **Recommendations**

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed to improve the hospital's operational efficiency and financial performance:

1. For partially paid accounts, the actual amount received and the remaining outstanding balance should be recorded separately to provide accurate financial information.

2. Management should introduce a structured follow up and debt recovery process to minimise revenue leakages.

3. The dashboard should be adopted as a routine management monitoring tool, to enable management to track and identify operational challenges early.

4. Management should use patient admission volumes and departmental billing contributions to guide staffing, equipment distribution and resource allocation.

## **Conclusion**
The developed Hospital Management System Dashboard provides management with a centralised and visual platform for monitoring key performance indicators, identifying operational trends and evaluating the hospital's overall performance.

Ultimately, the effective utilisation of the dashboard, alongside the implementation of the recommendations outlined above, will support improved financial accountability, efficient resource utilisation and more informed management decisions, ultimately contributing to better healthcare service delivery.
